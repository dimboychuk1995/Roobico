What Roobico is

Roobico is shop-management software for auto repair shops and fleets. It keeps your customers and their units (trucks, trailers, cars), the work orders you perform on them, the parts you stock and buy from vendors, the payments customers make, and the reports that tell you how the business is doing. Mechanics use the same system — on the shop computer or the mobile app — without ever seeing a price.

How a job flows through Roobico

{% call step(1, "A customer and a unit exist") %}

Every job belongs to a customer and (almost always) a unit. Create the customer once; add units as they come in. A VIN fills in year, make, model and type automatically.

{% endcall %} {% call step(2, "A work order is opened") %}

The office creates it on the web, a mechanic creates it on the phone, or the calendar turns an appointment into one. The work order collects labor lines (jobs), parts from the catalog, misc charges and tax.

{% endcall %} {% call step(3, "Mechanics work with timers") %}

Mechanics see the job without prices, run a timer per job, add parts and photos, and press Done. The office sees the job live and gets a push notification.

{% endcall %} {% call step(4, "The office reviews and invoices") %}

A manager checks hours and prices, sends the PDF or an approval request to the customer, and records the payment. When the balance hits $0 the work order becomes Paid and locks.

{% endcall %} {% call step(5, "Numbers roll into reports") %}

Revenue, parts cost, purchases and payroll meet in the General Revenue report; balances show who owes you; the Activity Journal keeps the history of every change.

Money in one paragraph

Money comes in through work orders — labor, parts and tax billed to the customer, with payments recorded on the work order. Money goes out through parts orders (buying from vendors) and payroll (salaries plus hourly time from the uAttend clock). The General Revenue report puts these together; Customer Balances shows who owes you and Vendor Balances whom you owe.
